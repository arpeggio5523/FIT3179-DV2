Dogs in Australia

Which dogs share Australian homes, where are they recorded, and how are breed preferences changing?

Start with dog sizes and local breed favourites, explore registration patterns across communities, then follow recorded outcomes for dogs in RSPCA care.

These datasets describe different places, dates and populations. National survey estimates, council records and RSPCA statistics offer separate perspectives; their totals should not be added together.

01 · Dogs at home

From size to local favourites

Size describes the dogs sharing homes nationally. South Australian records offer a closer look at breed preferences.

Australia · 2025 survey

Small and medium dogs account for 76%

Small and medium dogs each make up 38% of the published Australian size distribution. Dogs weighing more than 25 kg account for the remaining 24%.

These are survey estimates, not registration counts. The squares do not represent 100 individually surveyed dogs. Size is based on weight, not breed; some small dogs may be puppies. Weight categories retain the report’s wording.

Source: Animal Medicines Australia — Pets in Australia 2025 , Dogs section.

Highlight size

Each square represents one percentage point of the published size distribution.

South Australia · May 2025

Kelpies lead South Australia’s recorded breeds

Australian Kelpie and Border Collie lead with 18,107 and 17,353 dogs. Their gap is 754 records, compared with 4,289 between Border Collie and third-placed Labrador Retriever.

Source: Dog and Cat Management Board — Most Popular in SA , Dog Breeds - SA worksheet, 30 May 2025.

How to read this chart

Hover over a dot for its count. The top ten were selected from 2,738 breed and crossbreed entries representing 331,557 dogs. Crossbreed entries remain separate.

Dogs and Cats Online (DACO) records use the status “At Home”. This is a snapshot of recorded dogs, not new registrations during the year.

South Australia · 68 councils

Border Collies are favourites across more councils

Border Collies appear in the top five of 63 of 68 councils. Australian Kelpies and Maltese Crosses each appear in 47. This measures how widely breeds feature among local favourites.

The breed with the highest statewide count does not necessarily appear among the favourites in the most councils.

Source: DACO — Top 5 Dog Breeds by Council . The filename says 30 May 2025, but workbook notes say 30 May 2024; the snapshot date is uncertain.

How to read this chart

Larger rectangles and darker blue both indicate more council appearances. Hover for exact values.

Each council contributes five appearances, giving 340 in total. Rectangle area represents a share of those appearances, not a percentage of dogs. Each ranking position counts equally. Interstate Council and Outback Communities Authority are excluded.

02 · Dogs on the map

Location tells more than one story

Counts reveal where dog records are concentrated. Proportions answer a different question: where is owning several dogs more common?

City of Casey · August 2024

Berwick has the most dog records in Casey

Casey’s dataset contains 27,618 dog records across 29 suburbs. Berwick has 4,971, just over twice Narre Warren South’s 2,425.

Sources: City of Casey animal registrations and suburb geometry .

How to read this chart

Larger circles represent more records. Hover for a suburb’s count and share of the dataset.

Circles mark representative suburb locations, not individual animals. These counts do not measure dogs per resident. Records have a last modification date of 28 August 2024.

Townsville · March 2026

Townsville’s records span ten divisions

Townsville recorded 26,034 dogs in March 2026. Division 1 had the most records, with 3,332, compared with 2,005 in Division 3.

Sources: Townsville City Council — Animal Registrations , March 2026, and electoral division boundaries .

How to read this chart

One dot represents approximately 50 records. Numbers identify electoral divisions; the enlarged view repeats divisions 5–9 using the same dot value.

Dots are randomly allocated within each division, with totals rounded to the nearest dot. They are not actual addresses and are not restricted to residential land. Interpret the pattern at division level.

South Australia · May 2025

Multiple-dog ownership varies between councils

Among recorded dog owners, 41.9% in Adelaide Plains had two or more dogs, compared with 12.5% in the City of Adelaide.

Using a percentage allows councils with different numbers of recorded owners to be compared.

Sources: DACO owner data , “At Home” records, 30 May 2025, and ABS 2025 local government boundaries .

How to read this chart

Darker blue indicates a higher share of owners with two or more dogs. Grey means no matched data, not zero.

Percentage = owners with two or more dogs ÷ owners with at least one dog × 100. These are owner records, not households or all residents. Council refers to the owner’s residence. Interstate and Outback Communities Authority records are excluded.

03 · Changing preferences

Rank and count tell different stories

A breed’s rank shows its position relative to others. Its count reveals the size of the recorded change.

Registration series · 2018–2025

All seven groups finish below their 2018 totals

In the supplied registration series, Non-Sporting had the largest percentage decline between 2018 and 2025, at 56.1%. Gundogs had the smallest decline, at 15.0%.

Each cell compares a group with its own 2018 total. Orange indicates a decrease and blue an increase. Hover for counts and percentage changes. Groups combine several breeds.

Data: registration series, 2018–2025 . Original publisher and source URL need to be confirmed before submission.

Townsville · March 2023–2026

Miniature Dachshund climbs from 32nd to 13th

Maltese Terrier Cross remained first across Townsville’s March snapshots from 2023 to 2026. Miniature Dachshund climbed 19 places, while Dachshund moved from 15th to 12th.

Source: Townsville City Council — Animal Registrations , March snapshots, 2023–2026.

How to read this chart

Six focus breeds use distinct colours and symbols; direct labels identify their endpoints. Grey lines provide context. A smaller rank number means a higher position. Hover for values.

The display groups ranks worse than 35 at the lower boundary. Registration records do not necessarily equal the actual dog population.

Townsville · March 2023 and 2026

The rise in rank comes with more recorded dogs

Miniature Dachshund increased from 268 to 460 records, a rise of 71.6%. Staffordshire Bull Terrier fell by 299 records, or 22.9%.

Source: Townsville City Council — Animal Registrations , March 2023 and March 2026.

How to read this chart

Orange marks March 2023 and blue marks March 2026. Circles show 2023 and diamonds show 2026. Each line connects the two counts for one breed.

These six selected breeds are not the complete breed list. Crossbreeds retain their published categories. The endpoints do not show changes between the two dates.

04 · Dogs in care

Beyond registration: finding a home

RSPCA records add another perspective: the outcomes and year-end care status of dogs in its facilities.

RSPCA societies · 2024–2025

Rehoming is the largest recorded category

Rehoming accounts for 6,839 of 15,852 dog records, or 43.1%. Follow the ribbons from each society to its recorded outcomes and year-end care status.

These figures describe RSPCA care records. They do not track the dogs shown in the council charts above.

Definitions and coverage

Source: RSPCA Australia — National Statistics 2024–2025 , Table 4, printed page 7; definitions and coverage notes on page 9.

How to read this chart

Ribbon width represents the number of records. Colours identify categories. Hover for counts and shares; highlighting a society keeps the overall scale and totals fixed.

“Received” includes animals in facilities during the financial year, not only new admissions. “Currently in care” includes foster care at year end. “Other” includes deaths in care. Reclaimed and returned remain separate as published.

The report states that ACT, NT and TAS exclude pound animals, while the other states include them. Coverage differs, so these totals should not be used to rank society performance. They do not represent every Australian shelter.
