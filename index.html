Dogs in Australia

Which dogs share Australian homes, where are they recorded, and how are preferences changing?

Read each dataset separately. National surveys, council registrations and RSPCA records cover different places, dates and populations. Their totals should not be added together.

01 · Dogs at home

From size to local favourites

Size describes the dogs sharing homes nationally. South Australian records offer a closer look at breed preferences.

Australia · 2025 survey

Small and medium dogs account for 76%

Smaller dogs may appeal to households with limited living space, especially where owners prefer manageable exercise and transport needs. Yet size alone cannot predict temperament or care requirements. The survey suggests that practical household considerations may help shape the mix of dogs Australians keep.

One square = one percentage point.

Data: (Animal Medicines Australia, 2025)

South Australia · May 2025

Kelpies lead South Australia’s recorded breeds

Kelpies and Border Collies share a history of working with livestock in Australia. Their trainability and enthusiasm for activity may appeal beyond farms to owners who enjoy outdoor routines. Yet these traits also demand time and stimulation, reminding us that popular breeds are not necessarily suitable for every household.

Data: (Dog and Cat Management Board, 2025b)

South Australia · 68 councils

Local favourites differ between breed and cross entries

Maltese crosses appeal to owners seeking a small, affectionate companion suited to smaller homes and apartment living. Their adaptability makes them attractive to families, couples and older households. The “Maltese Cross” label also covers several different breed combinations, whereas “Maltese” represents a single breed—a useful distinction when comparing their appearances across councils.

02 · Dogs on the map

Location tells more than one story

Counts reveal where dog records are concentrated. Proportions answer a different question: where is owning several dogs more common?

City of Casey · August 2024

Berwick has the most dog records in Casey

Berwick's prominent total could partly reflect its residential scale and the number of households able to register dogs. Larger suburbs can accumulate more records without having a greater share of dog-owning residents. Comparing registrations against local population or households would help distinguish the size of a suburb from its preference for dogs.

Townsville · March 2026

Division 1 leads despite its more dispersed dots

Division 1 has the most dog records, with 3,332 in March 2026, compared with 2,005 in Division 3—the lowest total across the ten divisions. This matters because Australian research finds that people living in flats or apartments are less likely to own pets than those in freestanding houses. Together, these findings support housing type as an explanation for Division 1's higher dog registrations.

Data: (Townsville City Council, 2026; Townsville City Council, n.d.)

1 dot ≈ 50 dog records

South Australia · May 2025

Multiple-dog ownership varies between councils

Adelaide Plains and Adelaide council have very different housing environments. In the 2021 Census, separate houses accounted for 98.1% of occupied private dwellings in Adelaide Plains, compared with just 8.7% in Adelaide council, where flats and apartments made up 56.9%. This contrast in housing provides context for the differences in multiple-dog ownership shown on the map.

Keeping several dogs involves providing room for them to rest apart as well as time for exercise, training and individual attention. RSPCA guidance recommends that dogs living together have enough space to move away from one another. The demands of sharing a home with several dogs therefore extend beyond simply having room for another pet.

03 · Changing preferences

Rank and count tell different stories

A breed’s rank shows its position relative to others. Its count reveals the size of the recorded change.

Dogs Australia · 2018–2025

All seven groups finish below their 2018 totals

All seven breed groups recorded fewer registrations in 2025 than in 2018. Non-Sporting registrations fell the most, dropping 56.1% from 9,546 to 4,189. Much of this fall came from French Bulldogs, whose registrations declined from 3,095 to 675, and British Bulldogs, which fell from 1,381 to 429. Together, these two breeds accounted for approximately 63% of the Non-Sporting group’s total decrease.

The decline occurred against a backdrop of welfare campaigns about flat-faced dogs. The RSPCA and Australian Veterinary Association’s Love Is Blind campaign highlights breathing difficulties and other health problems associated with exaggerated features in French and British Bulldogs. These concerns provide context for the registration trend; the figures do not measure owners’ reasons for choosing a breed.

Data: (Dogs Australia, n.d.)

Health context: RSPCA Australia, 2016. Household ownership: Animal Medicines Australia, 2025.

Townsville · March 2023 and 2026

The rise in rank comes with more recorded dogs

The Miniature Dachshund's improved rank also reflects a larger recorded count, not merely other breeds moving down. Its compact size may appeal to some households, but smaller dogs still need suitable exercise, training and care. Negative stereotypes surrounding bull-type dogs may influence prospective owners, despite breed alone poorly predicting aggressive behaviour.

Data: (Townsville City Council, 2026)

Townsville · March 2023–2026

Miniature Dachshund climbs from 32nd to 13th

The Miniature Dachshund's rapid rise may reflect Australia's growing preference for smaller companion dogs. Rising living costs and smaller homes may favour compact breeds. Their distinctive appearance and social-media visibility also contribute to demand.

Data: (Townsville City Council, 2026)

Townsville · Reported dog complaints

Noise complaints fell, while aggression reports rose

Barking can reflect loneliness, separation stress, boredom or fear. Townsville City Council encourages owners to address these triggers and neighbours to speak with the owner, who may not realise their dog is causing a disturbance. Early communication gives owners a chance to respond before a dispute becomes a formal complaint.

When barking continues, Council uses a seven-day noise diary to record its timing and impact. This guidance explains how complaints are managed; it does not establish why noise reports fell between 2019 and 2025. The increase in aggressive-animal reports also shows that fewer noise complaints do not mean every type of reported problem has declined.

Data: Townsville City Council — Animal Complaints

Barking guidance: Townsville City Council.

04 · Dogs in care

Beyond registration: finding a home

RSPCA records add another perspective: the outcomes and year-end care status of dogs in its facilities.

RSPCA societies · 2024–2025

Rehoming is the largest recorded category

Rehoming represents a major pathway for dogs entering RSPCA care, highlighting the importance of finding suitable new households. Other outcomes reflect different circumstances, including owners being reunited with animals or dogs remaining in care. These pathways may depend on individual needs and available support, rather than simply indicating how well each society performs.
